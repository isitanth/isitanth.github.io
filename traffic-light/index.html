How it works

Car-following. Longitudinal dynamics use the Intelligent Driver Model (Treiber, Hennecke & Helbing, 2000). Every driver gets randomized parameters — desired speed around 50 km/h, time headway 1.1–1.8 s, comfortable acceleration and braking — so the flow is heterogeneous like real traffic. Trucks and vans are longer, slower and accelerate less.

The amber dilemma. When the light turns yellow, each driver checks the deceleration required to stop at the line. If it exceeds their comfort threshold (~3 m/s²) they commit and clear the intersection; otherwise they brake. Short yellow phases produce realistic late crossings.

Startup lag. On green, the lead driver reacts after a personal 0.6–1.4 s delay (occasionally longer — someone is on their phone). Followers start as the gap ahead opens, reproducing the start-up wave that ripples back through a queue at roughly 1 vehicle per 2 seconds.

Arrivals. Cars enter as a Poisson process at the rate you set, holding at the entrance if the street is backed up. Physics runs at a fixed 120 Hz with semi-implicit Euler integration, decoupled from the frame rate.
